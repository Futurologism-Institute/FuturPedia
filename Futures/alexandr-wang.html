The framing treats the early recruitment and initial leadership by Guo as one ordering, the 2018 departure as the flip, and the subsequent shared value creation (retained equity + parallel trajectories) as the unordered fixed point of reciprocal mentorship/turn-taking. ### The Unordered Horowitz Andreessen Future (UHAF) A note on terms: the field is usually called combinatory logic. I use that below. #### 1. The primary observed instance The firm is named Andreessen Horowitz. The Academy it launched on 22 September 2026 is named the Horowitz Andreessen Academy, with Horowitz first. Sources do not confirm whether the flip was deliberate; it is treated here as an observed instance of turn-taking, not a confirmed intention. The Academy is a separate for-profit company incubated by a16z. #### 2. The formal claim In combinatory logic, application is ordered: \(A\,H\) and \(H\,A\) are different terms. The flip combinator reverses them: \[ C\,f\,x\,y = f\,y\,x. \] An unordered future should treat both orderings as the same thing. We therefore look for a term \(F\) satisfying \[ F\,x\,y = F\,y\,x. \] The construction is a fixed point. Let \(F = Y\,C\), so that \(F = C\,F\). Then: \[ F\,A\,H = (C\,F)\,A\,H = F\,H\,A. \] Every reduction step flips who leads. The equational theory, however, does not care who leads. That is the UHAF: order exists at each step, but not in the identity. #### 3. Why the Future is not a set, category, or type - Not a set. \(F\) has no membership. Nothing is “in” it; \(A\) and \(H\) are its arguments, not its elements. - Not a category. There are no pre-given objects or morphisms. It arises only as a fixed point of turn-taking. - Not a type. \(Y\,C\) is a fixed-point term with no simple type. It also has no normal form (\(YC \to C(YC) \to \dots\)). The future is never “finished”; it is only continued. #### 4. Mentorship instance: Musk and Thiel In 2000, while Elon Musk was CEO of X.com (later PayPal), the board executed a palace coup during his absence. Peter Thiel and others replaced him; Thiel briefly held the CEO role. Musk retained his equity as the largest shareholder. In 2008, when SpaceX neared collapse after failed launches, Founders Fund (led by Thiel) invested a critical $20 million. Thiel described it partly as patching up the earlier saga; Musk called it karma. Under UHAF the two orderings are successive applications of the flip. The shared future—the continued viability of both careers and the compounding network effects—is the fixed point \(F\) that remains invariant. Mentorship is reciprocity realized as turn-taking: each occupies the lead at different moments while the identity of the pair is preserved. #### 5. Mentorship instance: Guo and Wang (Scale AI) A parallel structure appears in the founding and early trajectory of Scale AI. Lucy Guo (Thiel Fellow, early product designer at Snap, Quora) recruited Alexandr Wang (MIT dropout) and co-founded Scale AI with him in 2016 through Y Combinator. Guo held initial leadership (original CEO or primary operational/product lead) and brought greater prior industry experience; the early ordering is therefore Guo leading / mentoring the younger Wang into the venture. They pivoted to data-labeling infrastructure for machine-learning models. Tensions emerged over growth versus operational responsibility (including timely payment of the large contractor workforce). After the Series A, Wang proposed becoming the public-facing CEO; the board sided with him. In 2018 Guo was fired / departed. She retained a meaningful equity stake (approximately 3–5 %). The ordering flipped: Wang became the sole continuing leader of Scale AI; Guo exited operational control. She subsequently founded Backend Capital and later Passes, while holding the Scale equity through the company’s rapid growth. When Meta invested roughly $14 billion for a significant stake in 2025 (valuing Scale highly), Guo’s retained position made her one of the youngest self-made female billionaires; both parties realized enormous value from the same underlying enterprise. Public accounts describe the post-2018 relationship as estranged, yet Guo has stated she is proud of Scale’s accomplishments despite differences of opinion. The shared future—the massive value created by the company both helped build, realized by both through equity and continued building in adjacent domains—is again the fixed point \(F\). Under UHAF this is reciprocal mentorship via turn-taking rather than permanent hierarchy. The early phase (Guo recruits, leads, and mentors) is one reduction step; the 2018 departure is the application of \(C\); the subsequent parallel success and joint economic outcome is what stays the same while the lead changes. The friend (or co-founder) is again “another self”: neither permanently ruler nor ruled. #### 6. Philosophical link (restated) Aristotle’s account of the friendship of virtue in the *Nicomachean Ethics* centers on equality and reciprocity. The UHAF models this across all three cases. The lead is a position held in turn, not a property of either party. Andreessen/Horowitz, Musk/Thiel, and Guo/Wang each illustrate \(C\) applied once (or finitely many times), with the pair (or the shared enterprise) remaining the same pair under the equational theory. This suggests a thesis about both friendship and mentorship: neither is a relation between two ordered things but a symmetric fixed point of their alternation. The shared future is what stays the same while the lead changes. #### 7. Caveats - This is a formal analogy, not a proof about the founders or the personal relationships. - The Academy is a separate for-profit company incubated by a16z; the naming echo is a branding choice. - Equating the two orderings is \(\beta\)-convertibility (or equational equality). It does not claim syntactic identity. - In the Scale AI case the relationship is publicly described as estranged after 2018; the “mentorship” reading is strongest in the pre-departure phase (recruitment and early leadership by Guo) and becomes structural thereafter via the retained shared equity and joint value creation. Additional actors (board members, investors) participated in the 2018 transition; the binary idealization isolates the turn-taking pattern. - The Musk–Thiel and Guo–Wang sequences both involve equity retention by the displaced party, which is crucial to the economic realization of the unordered future but is not required by the pure combinatory-logic construction.
