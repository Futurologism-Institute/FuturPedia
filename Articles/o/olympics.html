Olympics

Also known as: Olympic Games
Governing body: International Olympic Committee (IOC)
Modern revival: 1896 (Athens); ancient precursors in Greece

The Olympic Games are the world’s leading multi-sport competition, held every four years in summer and winter editions (staggered so that an Olympic year occurs every two years). Athletes represent National Olympic Committees in sports ranging from athletics and swimming to figure skating, skiing, and many others.

The modern Games were revived in the late nineteenth century under Pierre de Coubertin and have grown into a global political, commercial, and cultural event as well as a sporting one. Related but distinct: the Special Olympics for athletes with intellectual disabilities.
