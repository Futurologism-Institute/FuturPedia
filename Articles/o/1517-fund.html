1517 Fund

Founded: 2015
Co-founders: Danielle Strachman, Michael Gibson
Focus: Early-stage capital for unconventional founders—often college dropouts, students, and deep-tech / “sci-fi science” builders
The name 1517 references the year associated with Martin Luther’s Ninety-five Theses—used by the founders as a metaphor for challenging credential gatekeeping (“paper belt”) in favor of builders outside standard tracks.

1517 Fund is a venture firm and community founded by the operators who helped build the Thiel Fellowship. It was created to extend that mission with grants and angel-to-seed investment, backing founders who are often under-credentialed by conventional standards but working on hard technical or scientific problems.

The firm describes itself as supporting dropouts, renegades, and deep-tech scientists at very early stages—sometimes before a formal company exists—and runs community and micro-grant activity alongside fund investing. Fund sizes have grown across successive vehicles since Fund I.

Relationship to the Thiel Fellowship

Strachman and Gibson were central to the early Thiel Fellowship. 1517 is often framed as “Fellowship 2.0”: the same talent thesis, expanded through venture capital rather than only fixed grants. Peter Thiel and other limited partners backed early 1517 capital formation.
