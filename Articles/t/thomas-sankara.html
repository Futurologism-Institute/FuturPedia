Thomas Sankara

Full name: Thomas Isidore Noël Sankara
Born: 21 December 1949, Yako, Upper Volta
Died: 15 October 1987, Ouagadougou
Role: President of Burkina Faso (1983–1987)

Thomas Sankara was a military officer, Marxist revolutionary, and pan-Africanist who led Burkina Faso from August 1983 until his assassination in October 1987. He is widely remembered for ambitious domestic reforms and a strongly anti-imperialist stance.

Key actions associated with his government include:

  • Renaming the country from Upper Volta to Burkina Faso in 1984
  • Mass vaccination campaigns
  • Literacy and education drives
  • Promotion of women’s participation in public life
  • Reforestation programs
  • Rejection of certain forms of foreign aid and debt

Sankara was killed in a coup on 15 October 1987. Blaise Compaoré, a former close associate, took power. Decades later, Compaoré was convicted in absentia in connection with the assassination.

Sankara remains an influential symbolic figure in African political memory, often compared to other revolutionary icons.
