Laws of Thermodynamics

Zeroth Law
If system A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B with C, then A is in thermal equilibrium with C.
Defines temperature as a transitive relation.
First Law
ΔU = Q − W
Energy is conserved. Heat added to a system equals the increase in internal energy plus work done by the system.
Second Law
The entropy of an isolated system never decreases:
ΔS ≥ 0
Equivalently: heat does not spontaneously flow from cold to hot; no process is 100% efficient at converting heat to work.
Third Law
As temperature approaches absolute zero, the entropy of a perfect crystal approaches a minimum (often taken as zero).
limT→0 S = S₀
Entropy Definition
dS = đQrev / T
For irreversible processes, total entropy still increases.
