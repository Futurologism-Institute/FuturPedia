John G. Trump

Full name: John George Trump
Born: 21 August 1907 (New York City)
Died: 21 February 1985 (Cambridge, Massachusetts)
Occupation: Electrical engineer; professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)
Family: Paternal uncle of Donald J. Trump; younger brother of Fred Trump
Frequently cited by Donald Trump as a “super genius” scientific relative. Core facts of his MIT career and technical work are well documented; some anecdotal claims (e.g. teaching Ted Kaczynski) are not supported by institutional records.

John G. Trump was an American electrical engineer who spent most of his career at MIT. He earned a doctorate in electrical engineering there (1933), joined the faculty in the 1930s, and later directed MIT’s High-Voltage Research Laboratory (from 1946 until retirement-era activity through about 1980).

Technical work

  • High-voltage engineering and Van de Graaff generator development with Robert J. Van de Graaff
  • Million-volt-class X-ray apparatus and applications in radiation therapy for cancer
  • World War II work connected to the MIT Radiation Laboratory and Allied radar efforts
  • Co-founder of High Voltage Engineering Corporation (with Van de Graaff and others), which supplied accelerators and related equipment

He received the National Medal of Science (1983) for contributions applying ionizing radiation in medicine, industry, and atomic physics.

Family and public mentions

As Fred Trump’s younger brother, he is Donald Trump’s uncle. Presidential and campaign rhetoric has repeatedly invoked his MIT stature. Independent profiles (e.g. Physics Today, contemporary obituaries) treat him as a significant but specialized figure in high-voltage engineering and medical physics—not a nuclear-weapons designer, despite occasional loose public phrasing.
