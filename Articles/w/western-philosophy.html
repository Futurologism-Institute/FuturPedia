Western Philosophy

Western philosophy is the tradition of systematic inquiry into fundamental questions of reality, knowledge, ethics, politics, mind, and meaning that developed primarily in Europe and later in the broader Western world, beginning in ancient Greece around the 6th century BCE.

It emphasizes reason, argumentation, logic, and critical examination of assumptions.

Major Periods

  • Ancient — Presocratics, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Hellenistic schools (Stoicism, Epicureanism, Skepticism)
  • Medieval — Augustine, Boethius, Anselm, Thomas Aquinas, William of Ockham
  • Early Modern — Descartes, Hobbes, Spinoza, Locke, Leibniz, Berkeley, Hume, Kant
  • 19th century — Hegel, Schopenhauer, Kierkegaard, Mill, Marx, Nietzsche
  • 20th century and later — Husserl, Heidegger, Wittgenstein, Russell, Sartre, Quine, Rawls, and many others in analytic and continental traditions

Core figures who shaped the enduring problems of the tradition include Plato, Aristotle, Descartes, Kant, and Nietzsche.

Large language models (including Grok, ChatGPT, and Claude) are computational tools that can discuss and generate text about philosophy. They are not philosophers and are not part of the historical Western philosophical canon.
