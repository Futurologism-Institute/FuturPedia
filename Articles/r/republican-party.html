Republican Party (United States)

The Republican Party is one of the two major contemporary political parties in the United States. It is often called the GOP (Grand Old Party). The party was founded in 1854 by anti-slavery activists, former Whigs, and Free Soilers who opposed the expansion of slavery into the western territories.

The first Republican president was Abraham Lincoln, elected in 1860. The party dominated national politics for much of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In the modern era it is generally associated with conservatism, free-market economics, limited government, strong national defense, and traditional social values, though its platform and coalitions have shifted over time.

The party’s formal organization is the Republican National Committee (RNC). It competes with the Democratic Party in elections at the federal, state, and local levels.
