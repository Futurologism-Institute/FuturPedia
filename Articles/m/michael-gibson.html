Michael Gibson

Full name: Michael Patrick Gibson
Born: 1977
Occupation: Venture capitalist, writer
Known for: Co-founder of the Thiel Fellowship (operations); co-founder and General Partner of 1517 Fund; author of Paper Belt on Fire
As of drafting, Gibson does not have a standalone English Wikipedia biography article. He is described on Wikipedia pages for the Thiel Fellowship, Thiel Capital, and Peter Thiel. This FuturPedia entry synthesizes those mentions and other public sources into a single biography-style page.

Early life and education

Gibson studied philosophy, including work associated with New York University, the University of Chicago (classics / linguistics), and a doctoral path in ancient philosophy and moral theory at the University of Oxford. He left academic philosophy before completing a conventional professor track, later describing himself as someone who expected to become a philosophy professor before entering Thiel’s orbit.

Thiel Capital and the Thiel Fellowship

In 2010 Gibson was hired to help Peter Thiel teach a course at Stanford Law School on philosophy, sovereignty, and technology, and to work as an analyst / principal connected to Thiel Capital. The day before TechCrunch Disrupt in September 2010, Thiel and colleagues developed the idea that became the Thiel Fellowship (“20 Under 20”). Gibson was pulled in urgently to help design the program and remained central to running it for about five years (2010–2015), alongside Danielle Strachman, who joined shortly after to lead design and operations.

At the Thiel Foundation he served as Vice President for Grants. The Fellowship supported young people who left or skipped college to build companies or research projects; fellows from that era include founders associated with Figma, Ethereum, and other high-profile outcomes.

1517 Fund

In 2015 Gibson and Strachman left day-to-day Fellowship leadership and co-founded 1517 Fund, with backing that included Peter Thiel and other limited partners. The firm’s thesis extends the Fellowship’s bet on unconventional founders—college dropouts, students, and deep-tech or “sci-fi science” builders—using grants and angel-to-seed venture capital. The name “1517” alludes to the year of Martin Luther’s Ninety-five Theses, used as a metaphor for challenging credential gatekeeping.

Gibson is a General Partner of 1517 and a public advocate of what he calls resistance to the “paper belt”—over-reliance on diplomas and institutional prestige as filters for talent.

Writing

He has written on innovation and technology for outlets including MIT Technology Review, The Atlantic, and others. His book Paper Belt on Fire (published 2022) develops arguments about funding outsiders and rethinking how technological and scientific progress is organized.
