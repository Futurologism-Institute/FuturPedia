Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)

The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) is a civilian foreign intelligence service of the United States federal government. It was established on September 18, 1947, by the National Security Act of 1947.

Its primary mission is to collect foreign intelligence, produce objective analysis, and conduct covert action as directed by the President. The CIA does not make policy; it provides intelligence to the President, the National Security Council, and other policymakers to support national security decisions.

The Agency traces its lineage to earlier organizations including the Office of the Coordinator of Information (1941) and the Office of Strategic Services (OSS, 1942–1945).

Source: Official CIA public materials.
