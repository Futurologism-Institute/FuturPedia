FuturPedia Acceptable Sources

Preferred and acceptable reference classes for straight (Tier A/B) FuturPedia articles. Satire and lore pages are not held to the same evidentiary bar, but must still be labeled clearly.
Companion to FuturPedia Inclusion Criteria. This list is ordered roughly by default usefulness for general encyclopedia-style drafting—not by formal academic rank.

Primary acceptable sources

  1. Wikipedia — Strong default starting point for overviews, timelines, and disambiguation. Prefer well-cited articles; follow footnotes to underlying sources when precision matters. Not a substitute for primary documents on contested claims.
  2. Encyclopaedia Britannica and comparable general encyclopedias — Stable secondary overviews for history, biography, and science.
  3. Official institutional sources — University pages, government sites, central banks, UN/agency profiles, company investor/about pages, program sites (e.g. Y Combinator, Thiel Fellowship, a16z Speedrun).
  4. Peer-reviewed journals and academic presses — Preferred for scientific, medical, and technical claims.
  5. Reputable reference works — Oxford, Cambridge, Routledge handbooks; subject encyclopedias; standard textbooks.
  6. Major wire services and established news organizations — For recent events and dated facts (Reuters, AP, BBC, and similar), with dates retained.
  7. Primary historical documents — Constitutions, treaties, speeches, official statistical yearbooks, court opinions, when accessible and relevant.
  8. National biographies and dictionary-of-national-biography style works — For historical figures.
  9. Official statistical agencies — Census bureaus, IMF, World Bank, OECD, national stats offices for quantitative claims.
  10. Archival and museum collections — For cultural and historical artifacts and documented provenance.

Acceptable with caution

Source typeGuidance
Corporate blogs / founder posts Fine for self-description of products and programs; not sole source for contested history or impact claims.
Think-tank papers Use with awareness of institutional slant; prefer transparent methodology.
Trade press and specialized vertical media Useful for industry detail; cross-check major factual claims.
Interview transcripts and oral history Valuable color; attribute as recollection, not settled fact.
Preprints (arXiv, etc.) Allowed for frontier science if labeled as preprint / not yet peer-reviewed.

Generally not sufficient alone

  • Anonymous forums and unverified social posts
  • Pure opinion newsletters without primary backing
  • Marketing copy presented as neutral history
  • Circular citations (page A cites B cites A)
  • AI-generated text with no recoverable underlying source

Practical drafting rule

For Tier A/B pages: start from Wikipedia (and its citations) or Britannica-class overviews, then harden important claims against official or academic sources. For living people and recent programs, prefer institutional pages and major news with clear dates.

For Tier C (satire/lore): sources are optional; labeling is mandatory.
