Legal Reforms for Space Exploration # Legal Reforms for Space Exploration ## Property and Resource Rights 1. **Clarify resource extraction rights.** The Outer Space Treaty bars national appropriation of celestial bodies but is silent on extracting and owning resources. An international framework, building on the Artemis Accords and national laws like the U.S. Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act, should define what ownership of extracted material means. 2. **Create a registry for claims and operations.** A public, internationally recognized system would record where and when companies and states operate, reducing the risk of conflicting activities. 3. **Establish "safety zones" with clear limits.** Zones around active operations could prevent harmful interference, with rules on size, duration, and what they do and do not imply about territorial control. 4. **Develop benefit-sharing mechanisms.** The Moon Agreement's "common heritage of mankind" idea has not gained wide support, but a more modest model, such as a small royalty fund for developing nations, could balance equity and investment incentives. ## Liability and Insurance 5. **Update the Liability Convention.** The 1972 treaty makes states liable for damage caused by their space objects, but it was not written for today's large commercial fleets, constellations, and in-space servicing. 6. **Set minimum insurance and bonding requirements.** Operators could be required to carry coverage scaled to the risk of their missions, including debris generation. 7. **Clarify liability for damage in orbit.** Fault standards for collisions between satellites are murky, and clearer rules would help resolve disputes. ## Space Debris and Sustainability 8. **Make debris mitigation guidelines binding.** Current guidelines (e.g., the 25-year deorbit guideline, now often shortened to five years in some national rules) are largely voluntary. 9. **Require end-of-life disposal plans and financial assurance** so operators can't abandon defunct satellites. 10. **Legalize and regulate active debris removal.** Under current law, a state retains ownership of its debris, so removing it requires consent. Clear procedures would enable cleanup services. 11. **Cap or coordinate orbital congestion.** Mechanisms like launch licensing tied to collision-risk assessments or orbital "carrying capacity" limits could manage crowding in low Earth orbit. ## Governance and Oversight 12. **Close the gap in authorization of non-governmental activity.** Article VI requires states to authorize and supervise their private entities, but standards vary widely. Harmonized licensing criteria would prevent "flags of convenience." 13. **Create a mission-authorization framework for novel activities** such as asteroid mining, lunar bases, and in-space manufacturing, which currently fall between existing regulatory categories. 14. **Strengthen the role of the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs** or create a technical body with authority to coordinate registration, deconfliction, and transparency. 15. **Improve dispute-resolution mechanisms.** Expand access to arbitration, such as the Permanent Court of Arbitration's optional rules for space disputes, and consider a specialized tribunal. ## Spectrum and Orbital Slots 16. **Reform ITU processes** to reduce paper-satellite filings that hold spectrum and orbital slots without being used, and to give new entrants fairer access. 17. **Establish use-it-or-lose-it milestones** for frequency and orbital assignments. ## Safety, Human Spaceflight, and Health 18. **Define legal status and protections for commercial astronauts and space tourists.** Informed-consent regimes, safety standards, and the end of regulatory "learning period" moratoria (such as the one the U.S. has repeatedly extended) should be addressed. 19. **Strengthen the Rescue Agreement** to cover private crews and clarify cost-reimbursement obligations. 20. **Set medical and occupational standards** for long-duration missions, including data privacy, radiation exposure limits, and duty of care. ## Planetary Protection and Environment 21. **Update planetary protection rules for commercial missions.** COSPAR guidelines are non-binding, and private actors may not be covered consistently. 22. **Protect sites of scientific and historic value,** such as Apollo landing sites, through a binding heritage framework. 23. **Require environmental impact assessments** for large-scale lunar or Martian activity, including dust, contamination, and light/radio pollution that affects astronomy. ## Security and Military Use 24. **Clarify the line between peaceful and military uses.** The Outer Space Treaty bans nuclear weapons in orbit but not conventional weapons or most dual-use systems. 25. **Ban destructive anti-satellite tests,** following the debris-creating tests of the past decade. 26. **Adopt norms or treaties on rendezvous and proximity operations** to reduce misperception and escalation risk. 27. **Address cyber threats to space assets** through shared standards and incident-reporting duties. ## Commercial and Contract Law 28. **Harmonize export controls** (such as ITAR and EAR) to enable international collaboration without undermining security. 29. **Develop a space-specific framework for intellectual property** covering inventions made in orbit and on celestial bodies, including jurisdiction over them. 30. **Clarify jurisdiction and law applicable on stations and habitats,** including criminal law, labor law, and consumer protection, building on the ISS Intergovernmental Agreement model. 31. **Adopt standard contract and financing frameworks,** such as the Cape Town Convention's Space Protocol, to make space assets easier to finance. ## Emerging Issues 32. **Regulate nuclear power and propulsion in space,** updating the 1992 UN principles on nuclear power sources. 33. **Address AI and autonomy in space systems,** including accountability when autonomous systems cause harm. 34. **Define legal frameworks for space-based solar power, in-orbit servicing, and on-orbit manufacturing.** 35. **Plan for settlement governance,** including citizenship, rights, and self-governance questions for long-term off-world communities. ---
