Friedrich Nietzsche (1844–1900)

Friedrich Nietzsche was a German philosopher, cultural critic, and philologist whose work has had a profound and lasting influence on Western thought.

Nietzsche is known for his critiques of traditional morality, religion (especially Christianity), and metaphysics. He proclaimed the “death of God” and explored the consequences of the decline of traditional values in modern culture. Central concepts include the will to power, the Übermensch (overman), eternal recurrence, and the distinction between master and slave morality.

His writing style is aphoristic, literary, and often polemical. Major works include The Birth of Tragedy, Thus Spoke Zarathustra, Beyond Good and Evil, On the Genealogy of Morality, and Twilight of the Idols.

Nietzsche’s ideas have been interpreted in widely divergent ways and have influenced existentialism, postmodernism, psychology, literature, and political thought. He spent his final years in mental collapse and died in 1900.
