History of Burkina Faso

Overview of major historical periods for FuturPedia reference.

Pre-colonial Period

The region was home to several societies, most notably the Mossi kingdoms (including Ouagadougou, Yatenga, and Tenkodogo), which emerged around the 11th–15th centuries. The Mossi states developed centralized political systems centered on the authority of the Moro Naba and successfully resisted large-scale conquest by neighboring empires for centuries.

Colonial Period (French Upper Volta)

France established a protectorate over the area in the late 1890s. In 1919 the colony of Upper Volta (Haute-Volta) was created within French West Africa. It was dissolved and partitioned in 1932, then reconstituted in 1947. Upper Volta became an autonomous republic within the French Community in 1958.

Independence and Early Republic (1960–1983)

Independence was declared on 5 August 1960 under President Maurice Yaméogo. A series of military coups followed: Sangoulé Lamizana (1966), Saye Zerbo (1980), and Jean-Baptiste Ouédraogo (1982). Political instability and economic challenges characterized much of this period.

Revolutionary Period under Thomas Sankara (1983–1987)

On 4 August 1983, Captain Thomas Sankara came to power. In 1984 the country was renamed Burkina Faso (“Land of Upright People”). Sankara’s government pursued ambitious social programs (vaccination campaigns, literacy, women’s rights, reforestation) and an anti-imperialist foreign policy. He was assassinated on 15 October 1987 in a coup led by Blaise Compaoré.

Compaoré Era and After (1987–present)

Blaise Compaoré ruled from 1987 until October 2014, when a popular uprising forced his resignation after he attempted to extend his mandate. Subsequent years included transitional governments, elections, and growing security challenges linked to jihadist insurgencies in the Sahel. Military coups occurred in 2022.
