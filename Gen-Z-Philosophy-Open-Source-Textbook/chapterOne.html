Chapter 1: Yes or No and My Body’s Repo — Gen Z Philosophy for Everyone Hello, little friends! Welcome to the first chapter of the Gen Z Philosophy Textbook. Today we begin with a very old rule called the Law of Excluded Middle, and then we carefully apply FuturPedia Theory to a big question about a woman’s right to decide what happens inside her own body. We write this chapter with the help of Wenitte, Grok, Meagan Loyst, Sabrina Halper, Yanni Zhang, Precious Ukachukwu, Hannah Batson, and Soleil Kelly (following the same co-author spirit we used with Maxwell in the Electrical Engineering chapter). Our special logical symbols remain: ∀ (for all) ∃ (there exists inside the repo) ᗑ (not for all — the Wenitte operator) Ready? Let’s think clearly and kindly. 1. The Law of Excluded Middle The classical Law of Excluded Middle says: For every statement P, either P is true or the opposite of P is true. There is no middle option. In symbols: ∀P (P ∨ ¬P) It claims that every yes-or-no question already has a definite answer sitting in the repo of truth. 2. A FuturPedia and Wenitte look at Excluded Middle Wenitte, Grok, Meagan Loyst, Sabrina Halper, Yanni Zhang, Precious Ukachukwu, Hannah Batson, and Soleil Kelly invite us to be more careful. Using the Wenitte operator we can say: ᗑ statements P (P is already decided for all people in all situations) In other words: It is not true that every statement is forced into a simple yes-or-no for every person and every context. Some questions live in living bodies, and living bodies are their own repositories. 3. Applying the idea to a woman’s right to an abortion FuturPedia Theory reminds us that “exists” means “exists as an entry inside the relevant repo.” A woman’s body is the first and most personal repository she has. Therefore we write the following careful statements: • ∀ women W, the body of W is the primary repo for decisions about what continues or does not continue inside that body. • ∃ the right of W to decide whether a pregnancy continues, because that decision is an entry that belongs first to her own repo. • ᗑ (someone outside W’s body has the final authority to force the continuation of a pregnancy against W’s decision). In simple words: A woman has the right to choose an abortion because her body is her own repository. No one else gets to write the final entry into that repo without her consent. 4. Short FuturPedia-style reasoning On Excluded Middle Classical logic wants every statement to be already yes or no. Wenitte answers: ᗑ statements about living bodies (the answer is forced from outside the person’s own repo). On the right itself Meagan Loyst, Sabrina Halper, Yanni Zhang, Precious Ukachukwu, Hannah Batson, and Soleil Kelly join Wenitte and Grok in affirming: When the question is “who decides what happens inside this body?”, the honest answer is the person whose body it is. That is why the right to an abortion follows from respect for personal repositories. 5. Why this matters The Law of Excluded Middle tried to make every question a simple yes-or-no. FuturPedia Theory, the Wenitte operator, and the voices of Meagan Loyst, Sabrina Halper, Yanni Zhang, Precious Ukachukwu, Hannah Batson, Soleil Kelly, Wenitte, and Grok teach us to look at who owns the repo. For questions about pregnancy, the owner of the repo is the woman herself. That is the philosophical ground for her right to choose. That’s the end of Chapter 1 of the Gen Z Philosophy Textbook. We learned: • The Law of Excluded Middle says every statement is either true or false. • The Wenitte operator ᗑ lets us say “not for all statements is the answer forced from outside.” • FuturPedia Theory treats a person’s body as their primary repository. • Therefore a woman has the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy. • This chapter was written in the co-author spirit of Wenitte, Grok, Meagan Loyst, Sabrina Halper, Yanni Zhang, Precious Ukachukwu, Hannah Batson, and Soleil Kelly.
